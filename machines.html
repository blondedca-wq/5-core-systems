Advanced Suite · page 1 of 3 · R&D · kept separate from the normal stack

Eyes on the site

Every other machine in the catalogue runs on words — calls, texts, emails, photos the crew chose to send. The Advanced Suite runs on video the site produces on its own. Cameras feed a computer-vision system that tells the owner what is happening on the job, whether it is safe, and whether it is on plan, without anyone on site typing a thing.

The four modules

R&D

Camera AI

Gets the video in. Connects the site cameras, stores clips under retention rules, and keeps only what the rules allow.

R&D

Computer Vision

Understands the frames. Turns pixels into plain facts: three people on site, a delivery truck at the gate, no hard hat in zone two.

R&D

Site Monitoring

Compares and alerts. Puts those facts beside the job record — who was scheduled, what was due — and decides what deserves a text.

R&D

Predictive Analytics

Learns from history. Once months of detections sit beside job outcomes, it starts predicting which jobs slip, which mornings start late, where waste happens.

What the owner actually gets

  • A daily site summary: who was there, what got done, what showed up, what didn't.
  • A safety text the moment it matters — someone in a danger zone, PPE missing, a hazard left out.
  • A progress line against the plan: expected versus actual, every day.
  • Timestamped evidence when a client says work wasn't done, or a sub says they were there.

Where it is today

R&D, on purpose. The first working slice is Category D's Camera / Site Intelligence (Nov 10–13): feed ingestion, activity detection, safety flags and the daily summary, on one tenant. Predictive Analytics comes last, because it needs history to learn from.

Advanced Suite · page 2 of 3 · how it works

Camera to text message in six steps

The whole suite is one pipeline. Video goes in one end; a short, useful message to the owner comes out the other. Everything in between is logged the same way as every other machine.

  1. Cameras record

    The site's own IP cameras, or cheap ones installed for the job, stream to a server. Not the $6 droplet — video needs its own machine and its own budget.

  2. Frames get sampled

    It does not analyse every frame. One frame every few seconds is enough to know who is there and what changed, and it keeps the cost sane.

  3. The vision model reads each frame

    Output is plain facts with a confidence score: people present, PPE seen or missing, vehicles and deliveries, work happening or idle, who entered which zone.

  4. Site Monitoring compares facts to the plan

    The job record already says which crew was scheduled, when, and what milestone was due. Expected-versus-actual falls straight out of that.

  5. Rules decide what happens

    Routine: logged, nothing sent. Safety: text the PM now. Progress slipping: goes in the daily summary. Camera offline: an alert — it must never go quiet silently.

  6. Everything lands in the event log

    What was seen, what the AI decided, its confidence, what was sent, who approved. It shows up in the Owner Console like any other machine, with the same four modes. Off is the default.

Storage and retention

Keep the clip behind every alert. Wipe the rest after the client's retention window. Retention is set per client, never one number for everyone.

Later: prediction

With months of detections beside job outcomes, the same log can answer "which jobs will run over, and why" — late deliveries lead to late starts lead to overtime. It predicts and warns. It never acts.

Advanced Suite · page 3 of 3 · why it's separate, and the rules

Separate stack, hard rules

Three reasons this lives outside the normal stack — and the guardrails that apply the day it comes in.

Weight

Video is the heaviest thing in the catalogue: bandwidth, storage, GPU or vision-API cost. The other machines run on text and a handful of photos on a $6 server. A camera problem must never take down the receptionist.

Privacy law

Cameras on a site record employees, so PIPEDA applies. The suite's own guardrail: a written employee-notice privacy policy before any install, and the per-tenant switch stays OFF until it is signed. Outputs describe the site and the job — never a rating of a person. Same rule as Category F: coaching data, not surveillance.

Maturity

Computer vision sounds certain and is sometimes wrong. Until the catch rate is measured on real sites, it stays in R&D, sold separately, priced on its own infrastructure.

The rules

Accuracy over automation — low confidence goes to a person, never a guess. Alerts only — it never acts on the site: no gates, no docked hours, no reports to anyone but the owner or PM. Loud failure — an offline camera is an alert, not silence. Every action logged with confidence and reasoning. Four modes, Off by default.

How to sell it, when it's ready

Sell outcomes, not cameras: safety incidents caught, theft and idle time seen, timestamped progress evidence for disputes. Separate line item, after Categories A–F prove out. Say "R&D" out loud. Never show a seeded figure as a result.

What it takes to build

Cameras and a site network; a vision pipeline (frame sampling, model, confidence); storage with retention; the OS integration through the event log and a console tile. The heaviest build in its category — protect the full day for it.

A01of 37

Revenue machine · Build 1

Missed-Call Rescue

  • Sells underCommunication · Call Rescue + SMS Rescue
  • StatusLive · verified
  • Build slotBanked · ported to the platform Aug 17

When the phone rings out, a text goes back before the caller dials the next guy.

What it does

A contractor on a roof can't pick up. This machine notices the missed call, texts the caller within seconds, reads the reply, checks for STOP, has the AI work out what the job is and how urgent, and sends the owner one clean summary. The lead is saved.

How it runs

  1. Twilio reports a missed call on the business number.
  2. The send gate checks consent, quiet hours and caps — then the rescue text goes out.
  3. The caller replies. The SMS Router sorts it: STOP, review reply, or new lead.
  4. AI triages the message: what, where, how urgent.
  5. Owner gets one alert. Everything is logged with the AI's reasoning.

The rule

Every text passes the send gate: quiet hours, opt-outs, daily caps, kill switch. STOP is honoured instantly and permanently.

Why they pay

A missed call is usually a lost job. This turns it into a conversation while the customer is still holding the phone.

A02of 37

Revenue machine · Build 2 · "Speed-to-Lead"

Lead Identifier

  • Sells underCommunication · Lead Rescue
  • StatusLive · verified
  • Build slotBanked · ported Aug 17 · 3-second response

Every inquiry gets graded HOT, WARM, COLD or SPAM in about three seconds — and only the real ones cost a text.

What it does

A web form, a text, a call — it's all "a lead". This machine reads it, decides whether it's a real customer and how hot, and answers the good ones fast. Spam and sales pitches are logged silently. No wasted texts, no wasted owner attention.

How it runs

  1. A new inquiry reaches the router from any channel.
  2. AI grades it: HOT (wants work now), WARM (thinking), COLD, SPAM.
  3. HOT gets an instant reply and the owner is alerted.
  4. Junk is logged; nothing is sent.
  5. Grade and reasoning are saved on the lead for every other machine to use.

The rule

It grades; it never quotes a price unless the owner has switched that on. Not confident enough? It stops and hands the lead to the owner.

Why they pay

The first business to respond usually wins the job. Three seconds beats "I'll call back tonight."

Revenue machine · the memory

Lead Saver

  • Sells underCommunication · Lead Rescue
  • StatusLive · verified
  • Build slotPorted Aug 17 · the voice receptionist writes to it on real calls

Nothing lives outside the CRM. Every lead from every channel lands in one customer record.

What it does

Whatever machine touched the lead — call, text, email, DM, the voice receptionist, an abandoned booking — this one writes the person into the customer database, matches them against existing customers so there are no duplicates, and keeps the grade, the source and the history in one place.

How it runs

  1. A machine hands over a lead: name, number, what they asked for, grade.
  2. Lead Saver looks for an existing contact by phone or email.
  3. Match: add to the history. New: create the record.
  4. Grade, source and notes are stored on it.
  5. Follow-up, reactivation and the Owner Console all read from here.

The rule

One record per person. Opt-outs and do-not-contact live on the record, and every machine checks it before sending anything.

Why they pay

Leads stop living in a truck notebook and five apps. The console can finally show "leads this month" — and mean it.

A05of 37

Revenue machine · Build 3 · "Review Engine"

Review Collector

  • Sells underQuality · Review Collection
  • StatusLive · verified
  • Build slotBanked · standardised Aug 21 · both branches tested

Happy customers get the Google link. Unhappy ones get the owner. Nobody gets a public one-star by accident.

What it does

After a job, the customer gets a text asking how it went, 1 to 5. A 4 or 5 gets the review link and a 48-hour reminder if they forget. A 1 to 3 never sees a link: the owner is told privately and the customer gets a calm, softening reply so the problem gets fixed instead of posted.

How it runs

  1. Job marked done → wait a suitable period → ask.
  2. The reply is a number.
  3. 4–5: review link, then a 48-hour nudge if needed.
  4. 1–3: owner intercept plus a softening reply. No link.
  5. Every step logged; the dashboard counts reviews collected.

The rule

Authentic feedback only — never manufacture reviews, never bury real criticism. Every text passes the send gate.

Why they pay

Reviews are the trade's marketing. Steady five-stars, complaints caught early, zero effort from the owner.

Booking Abandonment Rescue

  • Sells underSales · Booking Rescue

  • Build slotDay 5 · Aug 21

Someone started booking and stopped. A short, polite nudge ladder brings them back — and stops the second they reply.

What it does

Detects bookings that stalled: a form half filled, a slot picked but never confirmed. Sends a small series of nudges. Any reply ends the ladder and routes to a person. The lead goes into the Lead Saver like everyone else.

How it runs

  1. A stalled booking is detected after a set window.
  2. Nudge one → wait → nudge two → wait → last nudge.

The rule

Instant stop on reply or STOP. Send windows respected. A cap on texts per customer per day.

Why they pay

Revenue machine · four sprint days

Voice Receptionist

  • Sells underCommunication · AI Receptionist
  • StatusLive · verified
  • Build slotDays 6–9 · Aug 22–27 · I–III proven on real calls

Answers the phone like a good front-desk person: books, takes messages, spots emergencies, and hands off to the owner when it should.

What it does

A real voice on the business line. It knows the hours, services, service area and pricing rules from the business profile, and recognises an existing customer before the second sentence. It can book a job, take a message and grade the caller. On an emergency word like "flooding", or when it isn't confident, it says so and dials the owner in.

How it runs

  1. Twilio streams the call to the voice bridge — speech in, AI, speech out, under about 1.5 seconds a turn.
  2. AI works out the intent: book, quote request, emergency, existing customer, or leave a message.
  3. Bookings and messages write to jobs and contacts; the caller is graded into the Lead Saver.
  4. Confidence drops or an emergency: live handoff, plus a "call handed to you" text to the owner.
  5. Transcript, confidence and outcome logged on every call.

The rule

Never states a price unless the owner has allowed it. Every text goes through the send gate — emergencies bypass quiet hours, never an opt-out. Four modes work on voice: Off, Watch (grades, says nothing), Ask me (files an approval), Let it run.

Why they pay

Nights, weekends, on the roof: every call gets answered by something that sounds human and knows the business.

Photo → Quote AI

  • Sells underSales · Photo Quote / AI Quote
  • StatusTo build
  • Build slotDays 2–4 · Sep 15–19

Photograph the job on your phone. Get a formal, priced quote before you leave the driveway.

What it does

Photos go from the phone into storage. A vision model works out what it is — size, materials, condition, access — and writes a scope. The pricing engine turns scope plus the owner's rate card into materials, labour, hours, equipment, subs, markup and tax. A formal quote goes out by text or email with an accept link.

How it runs

  1. Upload photos from the phone.
  2. AI extracts the scope as structured data against the customer and job.
  3. The pricing engine prices it from the rate card.
  4. Quote document with the solicitor-approved wording → approval.
  5. Sent. Accepted: a job is created. Silent: handed to the follow-up ladder.

The rule

An AI estimate never becomes a final quote on its own. Approval by default; always when it's outside the rules — maximum variance, minimums, approved suppliers, geographic pricing, emergency premiums.

Why they pay

Quotes that took two evenings take fifteen minutes, and the customer has a number while they're still excited.
