## D001 — Target three blue-collar trades verticals

**Date:** 2026-07-30
**Status:** Active
**Sprint position:** Day 1, task 1

### Decision

Sell to three verticals only, in the GTA:

1. **Renovation · Landscaping · Roofing** — *anchor*. Job value $2,000–50,000. Lead build: Quote-from-Photo.
2. **HVAC · Plumbing · Electrical** — job value $300–2,500. Lead build: Missed-Call Rescue.
3. **Auto repair · Detailing** — job value $150–1,500. Lead build: Review Engine.

No fourth vertical before the Day 27 sprint review.

### Why

- **Trade credibility.** 5 years on reno and landscaping sites. On a cold call I can say I have framed, done landscape installs, and know what it is like when the phone rings from a ladder. Every competing freelancer is a generalist who has never held a shovel.
- **Shared buyer persona.** All three are owner-operated trades reachable through the same channels — Google Maps, HomeStars, Yelp, local BIA directories, Instagram location tags. One cold-call script covers all three with minor vocabulary swaps.
- **No duplicated build work.** Each vertical maps to a different automation already scheduled in the sprint. Three verticals, zero extra builds.
- **Price ladder.** $150 detail job to $50k roof means the same system can be sold at $500/mo and $2,500/mo without changing the product.

### The bet

That trade credibility beats technical polish on a first call — that "I have worked your sites" opens more doors than "I build AI systems".

### Known weakness

None of the three is recurring-revenue-native. Every pitch has to be framed as recovered lost revenue, never as a retention play. Accepted: recovered-revenue is the stronger pitch for this buyer anyway.

### What would make me wrong

- 20+ reno/landscaping conversations with zero interest → the credibility edge is not real, and I should reconsider anchoring here.
- Roofers consistently refuse to discuss price on a first call → high job value does not convert, and the ladder logic fails.
- One vertical produces every lead and the other two produce none by Day 20 → collapse to one and go deep instead of wide.

### Revisit

Day 27 (sprint review). The sprint already schedules "cut the worst-performing vertical, double the best one" — this entry is what that decision gets measured against.

### Follow-on notes

- Build order stays as scheduled (Missed-Call Rescue first, it is the easiest and most broadly convincing demo), but Quote-from-Photo gets the deepest effort since it is the one demoed with real authority.
- Collect old jobsite photos from personal phone now — before/after shots, material stacks, torn-up yards. These become demo and test inputs for Quote-from-Photo and cost nothing to gather today.
